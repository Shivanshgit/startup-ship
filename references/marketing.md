# Marketing Reference

Marketing without retention is a leaky bucket. Fill the bucket first.

---

## The order of operations

1. **Retention first** — confirm people come back after completing the core loop
2. **Distribution second** — now that the loop works, find more people
3. **Scale third** — only after distribution is proven, invest in scale

Most startups do this backwards. They spend on acquisition before retention works, burn money, and blame the market.

---

## Finding the first 100 users

### Community-led (highest quality, slowest)

Go where your target user already spends time. Be useful before being promotional.

**Steps:**
1. List 5–10 communities where your target user is active (subreddits, Discord servers, Facebook Groups, Slack communities, forums)
2. Spend 2 weeks contributing genuinely — answer questions, share useful content, don't mention your product
3. Once you're a recognized member: share your product with full transparency ("I built this because I experienced X problem — curious what you think")
4. Follow up with everyone who responds within 24 hours

**Works for:** niche products, trust-dependent products (travel companions, marketplaces), products where word of mouth matters

**Doesn't work:** B2B enterprise, products with no natural community

---

### Content-led (medium quality, compounds over time)

Document the build publicly. The audience becomes the first users.

**Platforms ranked by ROI for solo founders:**
1. **X/Twitter** — fastest feedback loop, developer/founder audience
2. **LinkedIn** — B2B and professional products
3. **YouTube** — highest retention, most effort, compounds for years
4. **TikTok/Instagram Reels** — consumer products, younger demographics

**What to post:**
- Build-in-public updates (what you shipped this week, what you learned)
- Problem-first content (describe the problem without mentioning your product — this attracts exact-fit users)
- Behind-the-scenes (decisions you made, mistakes, pivots)
- User stories (with permission — "this user did X with our product")

**Consistency beats quality at early stage.** Post 3x/week minimum for 12 weeks before judging results.

---

### Direct outreach (highest conversion, most time)

Personalised DMs to exact-fit users. Not mass. Not templates.

**Steps:**
1. Find 50 people who match your target user profile exactly
   - Check who comments in the communities from community-led
   - Check who follows competitors or tags similar hashtags
   - Check LinkedIn for job titles in the target user category
2. Write a 3-sentence DM: (1) why you're reaching out specifically to them, (2) what you built, (3) ask for 15 minutes of feedback — not to sell
3. Expected response rate: 10–20% if personalised, <2% if templated
4. In the call: demo the product, ask for honest feedback, ask if they'd use it, ask for one referral

**Works for:** any product where you can identify the exact user
**Doesn't work at scale:** too slow for consumer apps with millions of potential users

---

### Product Hunt (one-time spike)

A Product Hunt launch gets 500–2,000 visitors in one day if executed well. It rarely produces lasting growth — it's a spike, not a channel.

**Worth doing if:** your product targets developers, makers, or early adopters. Waste of time if your target user doesn't know what Product Hunt is.

**Requirements for a good PH launch:**
- 50+ people ready to upvote in the first 2 hours (morning US time)
- A clear, specific tagline (not generic — describe exactly who it's for)
- 5 screenshots that show the product, not the branding
- A founder comment explaining why you built it

---

### Press and media (high ceiling, slow start)

Works for products with an unusual story — not just "we built a better X."

A journalist will cover you if:
- There's a human story (founder background, underserved community, unusual use case)
- There's a trend angle (connect to something already being covered)
- There's data (you have numbers that prove something interesting)

**How to pitch:**
1. One paragraph: hook → what it is → why it matters now → data point
2. Send to journalists who have covered similar stories (search their bylines)
3. Follow up once, 5 days later, then stop

## Retention tactics

These come before growth. Don't run acquisition while retention is broken.

### Activation
The goal is to get users to their first "aha moment" — the moment they feel the core value.

- Remove every step between sign-up and the aha moment
- For social/marketplace apps: the aha moment requires another user. Seed the supply side first.
- Onboarding should complete the first loop, not just set up the account

### Engagement
After activation: bring users back.

- Push notifications for high-intent events only (new request, match confirmed, message received) — not for engagement farming
- Email digest: weekly summary of relevant trips (if applicable)
- Never send notifications users didn't ask for

### Referral
The most efficient growth loop: users bring users.

- Design referral into the core loop, not as an afterthought
- For travel companion apps: sharing a trip preview is natural referral
- Incentivise with access to premium features, not cash — cash attracts the wrong users

## Metrics to track

| Stage | Metric | Target |
|-------|--------|--------|
| Acquisition | Visitors → sign-ups | >15% |
| Activation | Sign-ups → first loop completed | >40% |
| Retention | Day 7 return rate | >25% |
| Retention | Day 30 return rate | >15% |
| Referral | Users who invite others | >10% |
| Revenue | Conversion to paid (if applicable) | >5% of active users |

Track these in PostHog. Set up the funnel before the launch day.

## What not to do

- **Don't buy ads before retention works** — you'll pay to fill a leaky bucket
- **Don't do PR before you have users** — journalists want a story with users in it
- **Don't build features for launch hype** — build for the loop, launch what works
- **Don't optimise for sign-ups** — a user who signs up and leaves is not a user
- **Don't launch to everyone at once** — launch to one community, learn, then expand
