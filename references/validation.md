# Validation Reference

Kill bad ideas fast. Most startups fail because they built something nobody wanted.
Validation costs days. Building costs months.

---

## The validation framework

Run these four checks in order. Stop and pivot if any check fails.

---

### Check 1 — Market signal

**Question:** Is there an active community of people experiencing this problem right now?

**Where to look:**
- Reddit: search `[problem] site:reddit.com` — look for threads with >50 comments and recent activity
- X/Twitter: search the problem phrase — are people complaining or asking about it weekly?
- App Store reviews: find the closest existing app, sort by 1-star reviews — what do users hate?
- Indie Hackers, Hacker News: search "Ask HN: is there a tool for [problem]"
- Facebook Groups, Discord servers in the niche

**Pass:** You find >3 independent communities or threads with people actively complaining about or asking about this exact problem.

**Fail:** The problem exists theoretically but you can't find people talking about it.

---

### Check 2 — Competitor audit

**Question:** What already exists, and why is it insufficient?

**Steps:**
1. Google `[problem] app`, `[problem] tool`, `[problem] software`
2. Search Product Hunt for the category
3. Check the App Store and Play Store
4. Ask ChatGPT: "What tools exist for [problem]?"

**For each competitor, record:**
- Name, URL, approximate user count
- What they do well
- What their 1-star reviews say
- Why they failed (if defunct)
- The gap they leave

**Pass:** Competitors exist (proving the market) but leave a clear, specific gap your thesis addresses.

**Fail — two bad outcomes:**
- Nothing exists at all → the market may not exist yet, or you've misidentified the problem
- A dominant solution exists with no clear gap → hard to displace

---

### Check 3 — Willingness signal

**Question:** Will a real person change their behaviour for this?

This is the most important check. Ideas feel real until you ask someone to act on them.

**Pick one test to run before building:**

**Option A — Landing page test**
Build a one-page site (Carrd, free) with the thesis, the core value prop, and an email signup. Share it in the communities from Check 1. Target: 50 signups from people you don't know personally within 2 weeks. Below 10: the message isn't landing.

**Option B — Direct interview**
Find 5 people who match the target user profile. Not friends. Not family. Ask:
- "Tell me about the last time you experienced [problem]"
- "What did you do about it?"
- "What would you pay for a solution?"
Do NOT pitch. Do NOT describe your solution until they've answered. Listen for the pain.

**Option C — Pre-sell**
Offer to sell the product before it exists. "I'm building X. Here's what it does. Here's the price. Want early access?" Real willingness = real money. Even $5 pre-orders from 10 strangers beats 1,000 email signups.

**Pass:** People you don't know personally show genuine interest with their time, email, or money.

**Fail:** Only friends/family sign up. Strangers don't act.

---

### Check 4 — Founder fit

**Question:** Do you have an unfair advantage in this space?

Unfair advantage is one of:
- **Domain expertise** — you have lived the problem personally for years
- **Network** — you have direct access to the target user community
- **Technical edge** — you can build something competitors can't
- **Distribution** — you already have an audience in this space

This isn't a hard gate — you can build without founder fit. But honestly assessing it tells you your biggest risk.

**No founder fit:** Your biggest risk is that someone with fit will out-execute you once they see traction. Plan for speed.

---

## Validation verdict

| Result | Action |
|--------|--------|
| All 4 pass | **Build** — proceed to MVP scoping |
| Check 1 fails | **Kill or re-scope** — the market may not exist at the right scale |
| Check 2 fails (no gap) | **Pivot** — find a different angle or underserved segment |
| Check 3 fails | **Kill** — the problem isn't painful enough to change behaviour |
| Check 4 fails only | **Build with caution** — move fast, distribute in communities you need to earn access to |

---

## What validation is NOT

- Asking your friends if it's a good idea → they will say yes
- Getting 10,000 Twitter likes on a concept tweet → likes ≠ users
- A market research report → reports describe the past
- Building a prototype first → that's building, not validating
